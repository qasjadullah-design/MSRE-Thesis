## What the demonstration shows, and does not

`python -m dcaccess`, synthetic system, site of 1,000 MW gross load with 700 MW firm, a 400 MW generator and 10% flexibility:

| Case | Site grid import MWh | Onsite MWh | Curtailed MWh | Involuntary unserved MWh |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Generator available | 6,132,000 | 2,628,000 | 0 | 0 |
| Generator out | 6,132,000 | 0 | 876,000 | 1,752,000 |
| Generator out, no flexibility | 6,132,000 | 0 | 0 | 2,628,000 |

It shows the accounting works: imports stop at the entitlement, flexibility caps curtailment, and the rest is reported as involuntary. The magnitudes are invented and mean nothing.

## Assumptions

- Site unserved load carries the same penalty as other firm load, so the model has no preference about who is shed in a shortage. This is the modelling treatment of firm priority.
- Voluntary curtailment is priced at the site's own cost and is used whenever the system price exceeds it, as well as in shortages.
- No export from the site.
- Adequacy is implicit (shedding costs the penalty). An explicit reserve-margin treatment is not implemented; the two options are in section 3 of the design.
